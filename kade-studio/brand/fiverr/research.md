## 1. Titles
- Always "I will" + verb pair ("design and develop", "create", "build") + **responsive** + the platform or tech + "business website" or "landing page".
- The platform is in the title because buyers search for it (wordpress, elementor, webflow, shopify). Kade builds custom code, so the equivalent keyword is **custom** / **HTML CSS** / **custom coded**.
- Target audience in the title: "for your small business", "professional business website".

## 2. Description structure (same skeleton everywhere)
1. One line on what they do and for whom.
2. **"What you will get"** bullets: 100% responsive, SEO friendly, fast loading / speed optimized, contact form, social media integration, Google Maps, premium look, cross-browser, secure.
3. **"Why choose me"**: experience, reviews, communication, on-time delivery, satisfaction.
4. Process in 3 or 4 steps.
5. **"Note: please contact me before placing an order"** so scope and price are confirmed.
6. What is NOT included (content, hosting, domain), to avoid disputes.

## 3. Packages
- Fiverr's help center itself recommends three tiers; third-party guides advise descriptive names instead of Basic/Standard/Premium.
- Basic = low-risk entry (one page or template-based), Standard = the complete business site (this is the one they want you to buy), Premium = more pages + e-commerce or advanced features.
- Typical contents per tier: number of pages, revisions, "source file", "responsive design", "content upload", "speed optimization", "SEO" (often Premium only or an add-on), delivery days.
- Delivery: 3 / 7 / 14 days is common. Kade's 2 / 5 / 10 is faster, which stands out, as long as it is kept.
- Prices: Top Rated sellers start at $80 to $250 for Basic; Standard $360 to $900; Premium $640 to $3 500. Vetted Pro starts at $195 to $600. Kade's 350 / 890 / 1 690 sits at the upper end for a new seller: fine if the gig looks pro, but the first reviews will come slower.

## 4. Extras (almost all of them sell the same ones)
Extra page, extra revision, source files, speed optimization, express delivery, SEO add-on, content writing, logo, hosting setup.

## 5. Gallery
- 3 images + 1 video. Image 1 shows the seller's work (site mockups on screens), not just a logo. Reviews mention "before/after" and real screenshots.
- A gig video is reported to get more impressions (Fiverr's own claim via seller blogs, unverified).

## 6. Profile and behaviour
- Response time "about 1 hour" is shown on the profile; top sellers keep it under an hour.
- Several gigs per seller (4 to 6), each on a different keyword: business website, landing page, redesign, speed/fix, maintenance. The cheap "fix / speed" gigs bring the first reviews fast.
- "Last delivery: 1 day ago" is shown; regular deliveries matter for ranking.
- Satisfaction guarantee and "unlimited revisions" are common at the top; risky for a solo seller, so use a high but finite number.
